[Slide 2]: Key concept  2 AI does not replace human judgement AI can retrieve,  synthesise  and generate at speed — but human judgement always applies. Knowledge Management ensures judgement is explicit, accountable, and applied before AI responses are trusted at scale. Trust is created through governance and insight, not control Consistency, confidence and limits are designed upstream, through validated enterprise insight — not managed through restrictions, disclaimers, or reactive intervention. Governance is defined with the audience in mind, anything destined for a client needs to be considered as high risk, always requiring an appropriate level of human involvement. [Slide 4]: Reducing AI risk 4 In an AI enabled world Knowledge Management means governing what knowledge we need, what AI is allowed to reason over and how knowledge is to be applied. AI amplifies knowledge risk, not technology risk. Poorly governed knowledge can result in client harm, IP loss, reputational damage and legal exposure. We need to be an organisation that speaks intentionally, safely and consistently through AI .  The EU AI Act introduces binding obligations for organisations that develop or deploy AI affecting EU individuals or markets, regardless of where the organisation is headquartered. Boards are not expected to design AI systems — but they are expected to ensure credible governance, risk control and accountability structures exist. Confidential Risk What this looks like How Knowledge Management helps 1 AI laundering AI-generated or AI-assisted outputs presented as original work without traceability KM ensures  provenance, attribution, and auditability  of knowledge and outputs. 2 Knowledge drift Incorrect or speculative AI outputs get captured and re-used as ‘truth’. KM embeds  validation workflows, curation, and expert stewardship . 3 IP leakage Sensitive data exposure or unintended use of copyrighted or non-Turner & Townsend owned material. KM enforces  classification, access controls, and safe AI usage policies . 4 Version chaos Multiple conflicting AI-generated variants eroding a single source of truth KM maintains  authoritative sources, version control, and lifecycle governance . 5 Context loss Knowledge stripped of nuance, assumptions, and proper usage context KM preserves  context, metadata, and usage guidance  alongside content. 6 Content sprawl AI dramatically accelerates the creation of redundant or slightly varied content. KM drives  deduplication, taxonomy, and structured reuse . 7 Bias amplification AI reinforces biased or incomplete knowledge already present in organisational data. KM introduces  diversity of sources, challenge processes, and governance . 8 Loss of tacit knowledge Employees defer to AI rather than capturing human expertise and experience, personal networks become fragmented. KM promotes  communities of practice, storytelling, and expertise capture . 9 Loss of trust Employees lose confidence in internal knowledge because they can’t distinguish human vs AI vs verified content. KM builds  trust frameworks, confidence scores, and content certification . 10 Regulatory risk AI outputs may violate legal, regulatory, or industry requirements (e.g. explainability, auditability). KM ensures  traceability, retention policies, and compliance alignment .

[Slide 8]: Stabilise and simplify the foundations 8 In an AI enabled environment,  quality is more important than quantity ; a vast amount of low-quality information is less valuable than a smaller, curated collection of high-quality managed knowledge.  Managing the quality of a smaller, curated knowledge set improves AI outcomes because AI does not reason like a human — it statistically predicts answers based on the signals, patterns, and confidence it finds in the knowledge it is allowed to use. Our goal is not to answer everything — it is to answer the right things exceptionally well. Confidential Signal clarity Large language models don’t assess “importance” the way people do.   They assess frequency, proximity, semantic similarity and confidence signals (structure, language, metadata). A well-managed, up-to-date knowledge base with reliable, unique content helps AI deliver accurate results, balanced confidence, and easier error correction. Reduced hallucinations Hallucinations are often caused not by missing knowledge, but by poorly bounded knowledge. A curated knowledge environment introduces productive constraint. AI is forced to work within boundaries, and these are more likely to trigger uncertainty signals and escalation prompts. Deliberate constraint makes AI safer, not weaker. Consistent outcomes One of the key risks is inconsistent outputs, this comes from knowledge architecture more than prompt design.   A smaller, curated knowledge environment explicitly resolves conflicts or contradictions, allowing AI to reproduce the same answer to the same question. This is essential for authoritative responses, legal defensibility and user trust. Explainability and auditability Explainable AI outputs depend on being able to say, ‘this answer is based on  this  knowledge, approved by  this  role and reviewed on  this  date’. With a smaller, tiered knowledge base, every asset can have clear ownership, confidence and applicability can be stated, audit and challenge become feasible. This is where KM becomes a risk and trust function in the business.

[Slide 10]: Design solutions for human + AI collaboration 10 Human + AI design adds value by  enhancing, not replacing, human accountability.  Clear roles, confidence levels, and feedback loops ensure safe, trusted, and scalable collaboration.  Thoughtful design prevents over-trust or underuse, helping AI boost insight, maintain accountability, and support better decision-making. Confidential Knowledge Management AI Human KM sets intent, AI accelerates access, humans retain authority. KM identifies what the organisation stands behind. By curating authoritative sources, assigning ownership, and  signalling  confidence, KM defines the boundaries within which AI is allowed to operate. AI does not decide what is important; it reflects the signals it is given. When knowledge is curated and trust‑scored, AI can reliably retrieve and  summarise  without amplifying noise or contradiction. Humans make decisions with confidence because the AI output is grounded in knowledge the organisation has explicitly validated. Accountability remains human, but effort is significantly reduced. AI supports thinking faster, humans still do the thinking that matters. KM ensures knowledge is  organised  so alternatives, constraints, and implications can be meaningfully compared. This is critical — unstructured knowledge produces misleading choices. AI is excellent at surfacing alternatives,  summarising  pros and cons, and identifying patterns across structured content — but it does not know which option is ‘right’. Humans bring professional judgement, situational awareness, ethics, and accountability to choose between options — especially where trade‑offs or uncertainty exist. AI exposes problems early, humans resolve them. KM ensures the resolution updates the system. KM establishes what ‘normal’ looks like and what happens when reality does not match it. Ownership and escalation paths ensure ambiguity has somewhere to go. AI is extremely effective at detecting conflict: duplicated guidance, contradictory statements, or deviation from standards. Flagging is a strength — resolution is not. Only humans can decide which interpretation applies, whether an exception is acceptable, or whether the standard itself needs to change. Speed without governance creates risk, governed speed creates value. KM manages lifecycle, applicability and confidence so that knowledge does not silently decay or drift in meaning over time. With clear scoping and currency, AI can respond quickly and appropriately — and refuse where confidence is low or scope is unclear. Humans remain accountable for decisions and advice, but they operate with clearer signals about how much reliance can be placed on the AI’s output.
[Slide 11]: Measure what matters 11 Trust and accuracy Focussing on the quality of the AI-generated outputs. AI accuracy rate  – the % of AI-generated responses that are technically correct and aligned with Tier 1 knowledge.  Measured through SME sampling. Hallucination frequency  – How often AI provides and answer that is not grounded in the knowledge base.  Measured by AI audit logs. User trust score  – The perceived reliability of AI outputs by the end users.  Measured through feedback mechanisms directly within the AI interface. Governance and readiness Monitoring the AI-readiness of our knowledge assets. Metadata completeness  – The % of SharePoint assets that all have mandatory AI-governance fields complete.   PowerBI  automated reporting. Knowledge freshness  – The % of Tier 1 (Authoritative) assets that have been reviewed / validated in the last 6/12 months.  Lifecycle tracking using metadata. Tier 1 coverage  – The volume of authoritative knowledge available to the AI.  Measured through inventory audits . Contradiction resolution rate  – % of detected conflicts resolved through editorial review. Productivity and value Proving the strategic asset status of KM. Time to answer  – The reduction in time spent searching for policy or best practice.  Measured by comparing average search session duration vs AI interaction duration. Expert deflection rate  – The reduction in low-risk repetitive questions reaching SMEs / helpdesk.  Tracked by monitoring volumes of ‘how / where do I? helpdesk requests. Knowledge gap identification  – The number of unanswered questions that lead to the creation of new Tier 1 knowledge.  Review AI logs for ‘no authoritative source’. Confidential Confidence score The combined score that informs how ‘safe’ our AI environment is at any point in time. Confidence Score = (% Metadata Completeness) × (% Knowledge Freshness) × (% Tier 1 Accuracy) High Score (e.g. 90%): The AI is reasoning over curated, current, and validated data. We can lean into more autonomous use cases. Low Score (e.g. 40%): The AI is reasoning over "fragmented or out-of-date" data. We must increase human-in-the-loop requirements and add stronger disclaimers.
